At first glance, maintaining a personal site no longer seems strictly necessary.

Projects can live on GitHub, updates can go to social platforms, and longer writing can always find some platform to host it. But I still want to maintain this site seriously, and the longer I do it, the more practical value I see in it.

## It Is Not a Resume Page. It Is Closer to Working Notes.

I do not really want my personal site to become a pure showcase page. To me, it is closer to a stable archive: a place for projects, long-term thinking, and judgments that are fuller than what social platforms usually allow.

Projects like `smail`, `hostc`, `workersai`, and `mlx-live` can certainly live in their repositories. But without a place of my own to connect the relationships between them and the thinking behind them, many things remain fragmented.

## I Need a Place Where I Can Speak Slowly

Social platforms are good for quick exchange, not for unfolding an idea carefully. Many things that are actually valuable cannot be said in one or two sentences.

Why I keep making certain kinds of products, why I am now researching edge delivery, why I move back and forth between cloud AI and local AI, why some projects need to stay deliberately small and clear. Those are all better written on a site of my own.

## The Site Forces Me to Organise My Judgments

The biggest difference between writing something as a post and keeping it only in my head is that the former forces me to organise the judgment.

You cannot only say “I like this direction” based on feeling. You have to explain why. You also cannot only say “this project is good.” You have to explain what it solves and where its boundaries are.

That organising process is valuable in itself.

## It Is Also a Relatively Stable Public Entry Point

The work I do is fairly spread out: developer tools, edge products, AI experiments, Go services, and systems research. Without one unified entry point, it becomes harder for other people to understand how those pieces relate to each other.

One meaning of a personal site is giving those seemingly separate things a shared context.

## That Is Why I Will Keep Writing Here

I do not know how large this site will become, but I do know it will not be just a static business card.

As long as I keep building projects, writing code, researching systems, and recording judgments, this place will keep growing. To me, having one place I can maintain over time still matters a lot.
