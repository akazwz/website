Winter teaches what the other seasons obscure — that stillness is not emptiness, and that waiting can be its own form of work.

## The Stripped Landscape

When the leaves fall and the canopy opens, you see the structure of things. The branching logic of a tree, the line of a hill, the true distance between one thing and another. Spring hides this. Winter reveals it.

There is a lesson in that. Clarity often comes not from addition but from removal. The noise quiets and something essential is visible that was there all along.

## Dormancy and Depth

The trees are not dead. They are deep in root work, sending energy downward, consolidating, preparing. Their absence from the surface is not absence at all.

Some of our most productive periods look, from the outside, like nothing is happening. The reading year. The thinking year. The year of long walks and early nights and conversations that go nowhere obvious. These are the root years. Trust them.

## Coming Back

Spring always comes. This is not optimism — it is biology. The question is not whether things will grow again but what kind of growth you will have prepared for.

Winter, if you let it, is a rehearsal for spring.
