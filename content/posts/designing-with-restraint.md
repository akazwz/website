The most enduring designs are often defined not by what they include, but by what they have the courage to leave out.

## Less as a Discipline

Restraint in design is not a limitation — it is a skill. A hard-won one. Every element added to a composition carries weight, demands attention, and asks something of the viewer. When too many things compete, nothing wins.

The great typographers understood this. A well-set page of text needs no decoration. The rhythm of the type, the white space breathing around it, the quiet hierarchy of size and weight — these are enough. They are more than enough.

## The Blank Space

Negative space is not empty. It is active. It gives the eye somewhere to rest, gives the mind room to think. Remove the margin and a page becomes oppressive. Add it back and suddenly the same words feel considered, authoritative, worth reading.

This principle extends beyond print. Interfaces, interiors, conversations — all benefit from what is withheld.

## A Practice, Not a Rule

Restraint is contextual. A baroque ceiling is not a failure of taste. A single flower in a vase is not poverty of imagination. The question is always: does this serve? Does this element earn its place?

When the answer is uncertain, leave it out. You can always add back. It is much harder to subtract.
